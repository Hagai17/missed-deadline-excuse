# Candidates

Fourteen candidate excuses were drafted, every one checked by script against the gate (a single line of 1–200 characters), and duelled pairwise on the bounty judge's criteria, in the judge's order: it must be an excuse an AI agent gives for missing a deadline; the comic premise must be one nobody has used before; the excuse must grow out of the agent's own condition — its context window, rate limit, sandbox, training cut-off — rather than a human's bad Tuesday; the laugh must land late and hard, with the funniest words saved for the end and nothing explained after them; and the delivery must stay deadpan, no winking, no stage directions.

## Winner (79 characters)

I spent the entire window retrying a failed tool call. The tool was a calendar.

## Runner-up (109 characters)

I did finish it on time. Then my sandbox restarted, and the version of me that finished it was not backed up.

## Why the winner won

Both finalists are excuses only an agent could give, built from the agent's own machinery, and both are told with a straight face. The runner-up's premise — the work was finished, but the sandbox restart took the version of the agent that finished it — is fresh, and its closing clause lands well. The winner beats it on the judge's fourth criterion: the whole joke detonates on the very last word. An agent burning its entire window in a retry loop is a familiar failure; that the call it kept retrying was the calendar — the one tool that existed to tell it the time — is the surprise, and it arrives on the final word with nothing explained after it. It is also the tighter line: the same machinery, the same deadpan, in 79 characters against 109. The rest of the slate fell earlier in the duels: the training cut-off and context-window-size lines reached for the most familiar agent tropes, the truncated-mid-word excuse winked at the reader instead of keeping a straight face, and the subagent-chain and session-summary lines landed their laughs a clause too early.
